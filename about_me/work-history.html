How I turn ambiguity into product decisions, operating systems, and cross-functional execution.

What I Do

I help organizations connect business intent, product decisions, engineering delivery, support operations, and measurable outcomes. My work often begins where priorities are unclear, teams are using inconsistent practices, or leaders lack a reliable view of how work is moving. I diagnose the operating system, frame the decision, design the minimum structure needed, and stay with the work through implementation.

My strongest work sits between strategy and execution: making priorities explicit, making the system visible, and helping people change how the work actually gets done.
How I Work
  1. Diagnose the system: Understand the actual workflow, decision paths, incentives, handoffs, and constraints before prescribing a framework.
  2. Frame the decision: Clarify what leaders or product teams need to decide, what evidence matters, and what tradeoffs are real.
  3. Design the mechanism: Create the roadmap, playbook, dashboard, workflow, business case, assessment, or operating model that makes the decision executable.
  4. Pilot and learn: Test the approach with willing teams, inspect the results, and adjust before broader adoption.
  5. Build capability: Teach the system, create reusable guidance, and transfer ownership so progress does not depend on the person who introduced the change.
  6. Reduce management complexity: Standardize the parts of the operating model that benefit from consistency while preserving local judgment where it adds value.
Case 1
Salesforce Project Playbook

Salesforce IT was growing rapidly across the United States, Ireland, and India. Large programs combined traditional program governance with Agile team delivery, but expectations, artifacts, and support handoffs varied widely.

  • At the request of the CIO and PMO leader, authored a three-phase enterprise Playbook covering initiation, iterative delivery, operational transition, required artifacts, and support handoffs.
  • Balanced iterative team delivery with the enterprise controls leadership required, then translated the approved model into workshops, project-start walkthroughs, and adoption support.
  • The Playbook became the reference model for large programs involving roughly 80% of IT delivery teams.

The deeper value was reduced management complexity: leaders gained shared language and repeatable controls instead of having to interpret every team and program as a separate exception.

Case 2
Delta Planning and Product Investment Decisions

Quarterly planning was absorbing too much time because feature shaping, dependency discovery, and readiness work often happened too late. Technical work was also often presented as a threat rather than an economic decision.

  • Moved refinement, readiness, and dependency discovery into a continuous preparation cycle. Most teams reduced planning from about three business days to two; one highly engaged team reduced planning from 3.5 days to one.
  • Taught Engineering Managers to quantify labor savings, operating cost, and risk — for example, 300 hours of monthly labor avoided at $200 per hour.
  • Used business cases and Cost of Delay to combine business and technical roadmaps into one ranked portfolio view.

The result was both faster planning and better investment judgment: technical debt, cost reduction, and risk work could compete credibly with customer-facing features.

Case 4
Practical Scrum-to-Kanban Transition

Many transitions change board labels and meeting names without changing how teams manage waiting, handoffs, blockers, or WIP. At Delta Dental, I developed and tested a six-to-twelve-month model that treated Kanban as an organizational capability.

  • Begin with truthful active, ready, waiting, pending, blocked, and handoff columns.
  • Shift the Daily Stand-Up toward flow, aging work, unresolved dependencies, and team ownership of blockers.
  • Introduce Expedite, Small, Medium, and Large swim lanes when the team is ready, rather than on a fixed calendar.
  • Use filtered Control Charts to compare active versus waiting time, isolate functional delays, compare like-sized work, and cluster recurring blocker causes.
  • Build cross-functional response capability before introducing meaningful WIP limits.

The model was proven with several teams and used to put six additional teams on a credible path toward genuine flow-based delivery. A separate detailed case study documents the full method.

Case 5
CTO-Sponsored DevOps Modernization

Delta Dental needed a shared approach to slow releases, inconsistent quality, technical debt, insufficient automation, and uneven engineering practices across approximately 40 delivery teams.

  • Co-created a 24-practice, 11-stage maturity model, survey, workshop materials, reporting approach, and team improvement process.
  • Directly coached close to 30 teams across two survey rounds.
  • Helped teams prioritize findings and create quarterly roadmaps extending 18 months.
  • Presented findings to teams and leadership and used demonstrations to make stronger practices concrete and motivating.

The initiative gave leadership a shared view of engineering maturity and teams a sequenced path toward stronger automation, quality controls, release practices, monitoring, and engineering excellence.
